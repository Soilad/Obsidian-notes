> Field of a toroid and of a long solenoid. Uses Ampere's law.

## Toroid (inside)
## $\oint \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{L} = \mu_0 NI$
## $B = \frac{\mu_0 NI}{2\pi r}$

Outside the toroid: $B = 0$.

## Long solenoid
## $B = \mu_0 n I$
## $H = nI = \frac{NI}{L}$

- $n = \frac{N}{L}$ = turns per unit length
- Toroid behaves like a long solenoid when its radius is large.

## Example
$L = 0.3\ \text{m}$, $N = 420$, $I = 18\ \text{A}$
- $n = 1400\ \text{turns/m}$
- $H = 25200\ \text{A/m}$
- $B_{vac} = \mu_0 H \approx 3.17\times10^{-2}\ \text{T}$
- With chromium ($\chi = 3.1\times10^{-4}$): $B \approx 0.03171\ \text{T}$
