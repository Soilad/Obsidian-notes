# Placeholder scaffolding
## Ethical communication
- Avoid language that stereotypes or excludes by gender, race, religion or political belief
- Use inclusive, neutral terms
- Represent others' positions fairly → the opposite of a [[Logical Fallacies|Straw Man]]
- Honesty about sources and evidence

## Cross-cultural communication
- Differences in directness, formality, hierarchy, personal space, silence
- High-context vs low-context cultures
- Non-verbal differences — gestures, eye contact, gestures that don't travel

## Different legal environments
- Data protection and privacy laws differ by country
- What can be said about competitors, employees, or governments varies
- Defamation and censorship rules

## Social media
- Permanence and reach — nothing is truly deleted
- Professional vs personal voice
- Audience is never only the intended audience

> Related: [[Rhetorical Triangle]] — ethos depends on fairness and good character, which is where ethics meets persuasion.
