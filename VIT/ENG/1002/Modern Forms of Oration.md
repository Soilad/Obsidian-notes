# Three modern forms
1. **Debate**
2. **Master of Ceremony (MC)**
3. **Commencement Speech**

# Debate
Formal, organised discussion where two or more sides present **opposing views**, support them with evidence, respond to opposition, and persuade an audience.

> The purpose is to **challenge ideas, not attack people.**

## Purposes
Examining an issue from different perspectives · presenting arguments · developing critical thinking · persuading an audience · testing the strength of ideas · responding to opposing viewpoints.

## Do's
- **Understand the topic** before speaking
- **State your position clearly**
- **Support arguments** with examples, facts, research, logic
- **Listen to the opposing side** — you can't rebut what you haven't understood
- **Acknowledge valid points** — a strong debater doesn't reject everything
- **Challenge ideas respectfully**

## Don'ts
- Don't attack or insult your opponent personally
- Don't interrupt
- Don't make claims without evidence
- Don't **misrepresent** your opponent's argument → that's a [[Logical Fallacies|Straw Man]]
- Don't use offensive or aggressive language
- Don't **raise your voice** — confidence comes from reasoning, not volume
- Don't rely entirely on emotions
- Don't move away from the topic
- Don't repeat the same argument — add new reasons instead
- Don't make exaggerated claims: "everyone knows", "nobody agrees"
- Don't let disagreement become personal conflict

# Master of Ceremony (MC)
Hosts and guides an event from beginning to end. **Not the main speaker** — a *link* between audience, speakers, performers, guests and organisers.

## Functions
Welcoming the audience · introducing the event · introducing guests and speakers · inviting speakers · making transitions · engaging the audience · announcing performances · making announcements · **managing unexpected situations** · concluding the event.

## Do's
- Know the **complete programme** beforehand
- Learn **correct names, designations, roles** — and pronounce them correctly
- Keep **introductions brief** and relevant
- Use clear **transitions** between segments
- Maintain **energy and enthusiasm**
- **Keep track of time**
- Stay **calm and flexible** when things change
- Conclude clearly and courteously

## Don'ts
- **Don't make the event about yourself** — the MC facilitates, not stars
- Don't speak too long between segments
- Don't mispronounce names or titles
- Don't be too casual for a formal occasion
- Don't make inappropriate jokes
- Don't criticise organisers or speakers publicly
- Don't panic at delays
- Don't interrupt a speaker
- Don't read the script mechanically — maintain eye contact
- Don't give personal opinions unless required

## Qualities
Confidence · Clarity · Organisation · Adaptability · Professionalism · Enthusiasm · Time management · **Presence of mind**

# Commencement Speech
Formal speech at a **graduation or convocation** ceremony.
Celebrates achievements, reflects on the educational journey, offers advice, encourages students into the next stage.

> [!note] Why "commencement" = beginning
> Graduation ends a degree but **begins a new phase of life**. That contrast is the standard exam question.

Delivered by a notable guest (artist, leader, public figure) or a top student (**valedictorian**).
