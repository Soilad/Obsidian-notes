# Rhetorical Triangle — Ethos, Pathos, Logos
From Aristotle's *Rhetoric*. **Rhetoric** = the art of using language effectively and persuasively to influence an audience.

| Appeal | Appeals to | Audience asks | Keyword giveaway |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ethos** | Credibility, character, expertise | *Can I trust the speaker?* | "As a ___ with 15 years…" |
| **Pathos** | Emotion, values, experience | *Why should I care?* | "Think about…", "Imagine…" |
| **Logos** | Logic, facts, evidence | *Why should I believe this?* | "60% of students surveyed…" |

# ETHOS — credibility
Built by:
- **Demonstrating expertise** — showing knowledge
- **Drawing on experience** — personal/professional
- **Using credible sources**
- **Showing fairness** — acknowledging other viewpoints
- **Maintaining good character** — honest, respectful

# PATHOS — emotion
Built by:
- **Telling stories** — personal or relatable
- **Vivid examples** — help audience imagine
- **Shared values** — fairness, freedom, family, dignity
- **Emotionally meaningful language**
- **Connecting with audience's experiences**

# LOGOS — logic
Built by:
- **Facts** — verifiable
- **Statistics** — numerical evidence
- **Examples** — specific cases
- **Comparisons**
- **Cause-and-effect**
- **Reasoning** — clear logical sequence
- **Evidence from reliable sources**

# Same product, three appeals
Smartphone "Nova X":
- **Ethos:** "Designed and tested by leading smartphone engineers…" → *experts stand behind it*
- **Pathos:** "Capture every laugh, every celebration… keeps your memories close." → *holds the moments that matter*
- **Logos:** "256 GB storage, 50 MP camera, 5,000 mAh battery — ₹24,999." → *the numbers make it sensible*

> [!tip] Answer template for "Identify the appeal"
> 1. Name it. 2. Quote the exact phrase that proves it. 3. Explain what that phrase does to the audience. 4. Close with the key idea.
>
> *"Ethos is evident here, as the speaker establishes credibility… The phrase **'cybersecurity professional'** establishes expertise… **'more than fifteen years'** demonstrates experience… Therefore the persuasive force comes mainly from the speaker's professional knowledge and authority."*

> Always quote the phrase. Marks come from the quotation, not the label.
