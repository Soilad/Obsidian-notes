# Two types
| Type | Also called | To whom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Informal** | Personal letters | Relatives, family, friends, acquaintances. May have no concrete reason — aim is a personal memoir |
| **Formal** | Official letters | Officials, business associates. Formal language, stipulated format |

Formal includes: official letters, business letters, applications, letters to editors, complaints.

# Three formats
| Format | Layout |
| --- | --- |
| **Indented (Traditional)** | From-address and To-address at left margin; **date, complimentary close, signature at right**. Subject and body indented 5 spaces. **Punctuation used** in addresses, after salutation and close |
| **Blocked** | Everything at left margin **except the date** (right). Punctuation omitted except in body |
| **Fully blocked** | **Everything** including the date at left margin. Punctuation omitted except in body |

> **Fully blocked is the current standard** for both formal and informal letters. Use it unless told otherwise.

# Five parts
## 1. Heading
Sender's address, **date**, receiver's address.
> Write the date **in full**: `10 March 2019`
> ❌ 10th March. 2019 · 10-3-2019 · 10/3/2019

## 2. Salutation
**Formal**
- `Dear Sir/Madam` — standard when you don't know the name or gender
- `Dear Sir` / `Dear Madam` — gender known, name unknown
- `Dear Mr. Sharma` / `Dear Ms. Iyer` — **more professional** when you know the name

**Informal** — My dear father, Dear Tulasi, Dear friend

## 3. Subject (formal only)
Short. Only tells the reader the **purpose**.
> Request for two days leave. · Information regarding spoken English course. · Remedy for pot holes on the roads.

## 4. Body — three paragraphs
1. **Beginning** — introduce yourself and the purpose, briefly
2. **Main content** — details of the matter
3. **Ending** — what you expect

## 5. Subscription (complimentary close)
| To | Close |
| --- | --- |
| Officials | **Yours faithfully** |
| Principal / Headmaster / Teacher | **Yours obediently** |
| Strangers | **Yours truly** |
| Blood relations | Yours lovingly / Yours affectionately |
| Friends | Yours sincerely / Yours lovingly |

> [!danger] Never write `Your's`
> No apostrophe with *yours*. Guaranteed mark lost.

**Polite leave-taking expressions**
- Formal: Thanking you · Hoping to be favoured with an early reply · Thanking you in advance
- Informal: With best wishes · With love · With kind regards

## Signature line
Signature below the subscription; may include a second line for title/designation.

## Superscription
The address **on the envelope**, written clearly with pin code. *Not required for formal letters* — it's already under the receiver's address.
