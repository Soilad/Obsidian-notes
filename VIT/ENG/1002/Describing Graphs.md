# Data visualization
Presenting information/numbers in **visual form** — graphs, charts, diagrams, tables, infographics. Readers grasp **patterns, comparisons, relationships and trends** faster than from raw numbers.

**Why it matters:** understand large amounts quickly · identify trends and patterns · compare categories · spot increases/decreases · communicate clearly · support arguments and decision-making.

# Chart types
| Chart | Shows | Axes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bar graph** | Comparison across categories | X = categories (or timeline), Y = quantity. Bar length ∝ value |
| **Line graph** | How a quantity **changes over time** | X = independent (time), Y = dependent. Points joined by line segments |
| **Pie chart** | How a **whole divides into parts** | Circle = 100%; slice size = proportion |

# What to look for
Title · Axes · **Units** (%, ₹, kg?) · Categories · **Highest and lowest values** · Trends (rising/falling/stable) · **Significant changes** (sudden jumps) · Similarities and differences · Overall pattern.

> Don't list every number. Identify the **main features, trends and comparisons**. Explain what the visual *communicates*, not just what you see.

# ⭐ Four-step structure
## 1. Introduction — state what the chart shows
> The chart **illustrates** / **shows** / **presents information about** / **compares** / **represents** / **provides data on**…

## 2. Overall picture — the most noticeable feature
Highest and lowest values · overall increase or decrease · largest/smallest proportion · significant differences · similarities · fluctuations.

## 3. Key details — figures and comparisons
**High values**
> The highest figure was… · X recorded the largest value… · X accounted for the largest proportion…

**Low values**
> The lowest figure was… · X had the smallest proportion…

**Comparison**
> X was higher than Y. · X was considerably lower than Y. · X was almost twice as high as Y. · In contrast,… · Similarly,… · Compared with X, Y…

## 4. Conclude — one-line summary
> **Overall, the chart indicates that**… · In general,… · It can be observed that… · The data suggests that…

# Six questions to answer
1. What is the graphic about?
2. What is its purpose?
3. What information does it present?
4. What are the most important elements?
5. How are the elements connected?
6. **What conclusion can be drawn?**

> [!warning] PYQ pattern
> "Observe the graph and describe it in 150 words" — **10 marks**. Also appears as "summarise the infographic in 100–150 words" plus short questions on it. Follow the four steps in order; the examiner is looking for intro + overview + data + conclusion.
