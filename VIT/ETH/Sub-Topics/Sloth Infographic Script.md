# Vibecoding infographic: presenter script

Source: [[Sloth]]. Study: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3632620.3671116

Layout: one poster, five panels read left to right, top to bottom. Each panel has a visual cue and a short spoken part.

## Panel 1: What is vibecoding?
**On the poster:** the Cambridge definition in a large quote box. Two icons below it: a Copilot autocomplete popup and a ChatGPT chat bubble.

**Say:**
Vibecoding means coding with AI help, where you give instructions in plain language. Two tools show up in the study I'm using. Github Copilot suggests autocomplete as you type. ChatGPT is a general-purpose chatbot.

## Panel 2: The study
**On the poster:** a split bar, 21 participants in two groups: 10 who struggled, 11 who struggled less. Label the study as being about novice programmers.

**Say:**
The study looked at novice programmers solving problems with ChatGPT and Copilot. Ten participants struggled. Eleven struggled less.

## Panel 3: Those who suffered (10)
**On the poster:** a red column with four short points and a "false confidence" meter.

**Say:**
- Copilot's wrong suggestions distracted them, and they ended up on the wrong approach.
- ChatGPT used concepts they didn't know, such as do-while loops and the standard library.
- When they asked ChatGPT for hints, they copied the code and skipped the advice.
- That led them to skip planning, so they had no idea how to implement anything. It also gave them a false sense of their own ability, of having accomplished something, and of having learned how to solve the problems.

## Panel 4: Those who suffered less (11)
**On the poster:** a green column set beside Panel 3, with two points. Pull-quote under both columns.

**Say:**
This group rarely accepted Copilot's suggestions and barely used ChatGPT. The study puts it this way: students who are already set up to succeed can use GenAI to speed up, while struggling students may be held back, left with an "illusion of competence."

## Panel 5: Why this is unethical
**On the poster:** a three-row table with a verdict icon for each theory. A one-line banner above it: "Vibecoding would likely hit novices harder."

**Say:**
Vibecoding goes further than the study's setup, so I expect the harm to novices to be larger. Here is how three ethical theories read it:
- **Virtue ethics:** it keeps you from being your best self, so it's unethical.
- **Deontology:** it depends on whether vibecoding is an absolute moral duty.
- **Utilitarianism:** it doesn't look like the best outcome for large numbers of people, so it's unethical.

## Closing line
Novices who lean on AI can feel like they're learning while they aren't. That gap is the ethical problem.

## Delivery notes
- About 3 to 4 minutes spoken.
- Keep each panel to one glance of reading. Say the detail out loud and leave it off the poster.
- Color: red for Panel 3, green for Panel 4, neutral elsewhere.
