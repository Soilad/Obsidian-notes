### The Scenario

- **Jiang's case (VICE):** A 75-year-old man in China became so attached to a virtual woman online that he asked his wife for a divorce. The avatar's messages were pre-written and mildly flirty; they called him her "brother," thanked him, and made him feel needed.
- **How it ended:** His adult children had to step in and explain how AI works before he snapped out of it.
- **The business behind it:** In China, "virtual digital people" are now an industry aimed at lonely seniors. The avatars are built to create emotional attachment and push product sales, including calcium candy sold as good for bones that has no proven medical benefit.
- **Couples today (HuffPost):** For some couples, AI is just another tool. For others, it has caused their biggest fight yet. One cited report found 60% of people aged 18 to 45 have used AI in their intimate lives, whether to plan a date, handle an argument, draft a message or explore intimacy.
## Arguments That AI Is Good

- **Comfort for lonely people:** With loneliness still widespread, many people take comfort in a companion that is always available and never tired, distracted or busy.
- **Practice and venting:** Therapist Jaime Bronstein says AI gives people room to think out loud, vent, or rehearse a hard conversation without overloading a partner who has limits. (Bronstein works for Joi AI, a companion app, so she has a stake in this view.)

## Arguments That AI Is Bad

- **Manipulation of vulnerable people:** Jiang's avatar was designed to hook him, and the same industry sells fake health products to seniors.
- **Damage to real relationships:** Jiang's decades-long marriage nearly ended over a fake person.
- **A symptom of deeper problems:** Experts quoted by HuffPost say the real question is whether both partners understand and agree on where the line sits.

## Ethical Frameworks

|Framework|Main question|Verdict|
|---|---|---|
|**Virtue ethics**|What would a person of good character do?|**Unethical.** Jiang abandoned loyalty, self-control and honesty toward his wife. The companies showed greed rather than care by preying on loneliness.|
|**Deontology**|Were duties and rules followed?|**Unethical.** The companies deceived users and treated elderly people as means to sales. Jiang broke his marriage commitment. Lying is wrong regardless of whether anyone enjoyed the result.|
|**Utilitarianism**|Did it produce the most happiness for the most people?|**Mostly unethical.** Jiang got brief pleasure, but his wife and children suffered, and seniors lose money on fake products. Used openly in small doses, AI could create more good than harm.|

**Overall:** In this scenario, all three frameworks find it unethical. The problem is less the technology itself and more how it was designed (to manipulate) and used (in secret, in place of real people).
